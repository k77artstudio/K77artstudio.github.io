I.

How resale works

K77 places no waiting period on resale. A collector may sell whenever they choose. What changes is how much support — and value — comes with the sale, depending on the path taken.

Path one — self-sourced sale

The collector finds their own buyer and agrees a price directly. K77 charges no commission on the sale itself, but the transfer must still be reported to the studio so the Collector ID and provenance record can be updated. A 5% registration fee applies, covering authentication and the provenance update.

Path two — studio-facilitated sale

The collector asks K77 to find the next buyer. The studio assesses fair value — based on original price, time held, collector demand, and condition — then sources a buyer from its collector network. Active sourcing runs for an initial three-month window; if no buyer is found in that time, K77 and the seller revisit together, either adjusting the asking price or continuing the search with no forced timeline.

On sale, K77 takes a 15% commission, covering valuation, buyer sourcing, and the provenance transfer — the collector arranges nothing themselves.

Path three — transfer outside a sale

A piece can also change hands as a gift or inheritance, without a sale taking place. In these cases, simply notify the studio so the Collector ID and provenance record can be updated to reflect the new owner, at no charge.

Why registering every transfer matters

An unregistered piece can't be authenticated, valued, or resold through K77 later — verification is what makes its history, and its value, real.

II.

Delivery & damage guarantee

Every K77 piece is covered by a one-time reproduction guarantee for two years from the date of delivery. If the piece is damaged or broken during this period — whether in transit or afterward, in the collector's care — K77 will reproduce the piece once at no cost for the artwork itself. Delivery of the reproduction is the collector's responsibility.

This is a separate two-year period from resale eligibility below — one covers the piece's condition, the other covers when it can change hands. Both begin at delivery, but they run independently.

III.

On value, honestly

K77 does not promise a fixed return — no serious collector's market does. What K77 offers instead is a studio actively managing scarcity, provenance, and demand on the collector's behalf, so value has room to grow rather than being left to chance.

As a general guide, and market conditions permitting, pieces resold after the two-year eligibility mark are expected to trend toward 10–20% above their original price. This is a guide, not a guarantee, and will sharpen as more of the collection changes hands.

IV.

Why this, and not a certificate on paper

A paper certificate can be lost, forged, or forgotten. The K77 Chain is a living record — every piece's history lives digitally, tied to a Collector ID that updates with each transfer, viewable at any time.

A digital token trades on speculation and can be copied endlessly. A K77 piece is one physical object with one verified history — real scarcity, a permanent record, and a studio that stays with the work.

Your name stays on record — permanently

Registering a transfer doesn't erase your place in a piece's history — it adds to it. Once you acquire a K77 piece, you are permanently recorded on the K77 Chain as its first ever collector. That record never changes, even after the piece is resold and moves to a new owner. Selling it doesn't take that away from you — you'll always be the one who found it first.
